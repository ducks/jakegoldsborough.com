Last year, my first trip to Europe was one week in Prague. This year I decided
to make a little more of it. I am spending three weeks in Europe, starting in
Madrid before heading to Seville for the annual Discourse company meetup.

I have been away from home for one week now. This is part one.

### Portland to Madrid

I left Portland on September 20th and flew to Madrid through Dallas. I had only
slept a few hours the night before because I thought being exhausted would help
me sleep on the plane. That did not work nearly as well as I hoped.

We were already an hour late when we landed in Madrid, then it took another two
hours to get off the plane and through customs. I had planned to take the train
from the airport, but by then I was mostly trying to get to the hotel.

I did manage to get a Madrid Metro card. I learned later that I could have just
tapped to pay.

I stayed at Hotel Artrip for three nights. The first day I got there, honestly,
I slept through most of it.

### Madrid might be the city that never sleeps

On my second day, I went to the center around Puerta del Sol and checked out
the official Metro de Madrid store. It is a whole store for Madrid transit
merchandise, which is exactly the sort of thing I will seek out while visiting
a new city.

Madrid is a unique city. It might be the real city that never sleeps. It was
busier at 3:30 in the morning outside my hotel than it was at 10 in the
morning. I am not sure I could ever get used to the flow there.

For a person who likes documenting trips, I took exactly two pictures in
Madrid. This was one of them:

![A tiled Restauración y Diseño sign on a building in Madrid](/images/2026/europe/madrid-restauracion-y-diseno.jpg)

On my last night, I met up with a coworker for pizza and gelato around Gran
Vía, then took the Metro back to the hotel. I could not sleep and stayed up far
too late even though I had a 9 a.m. train the next morning.

### A much better way to travel

The other picture I took was at Madrid-Puerta de Atocha while waiting for the
train to Seville.

![Renfe trains in the morning light at Madrid-Puerta de Atocha station](/images/2026/europe/madrid-atocha-trains.jpg)

The trip was scheduled for two hours and fifty minutes, but we arrived about
ten minutes early. No one bought the seat next to me, which was a nice
surprise. It was comfortable, easy, and made me wish the U.S. had a better rail
system.

### The long way to Triana

I went straight to Only YOU Hotel Sevilla and took a little siesta. After I got
up, I walked around a bit and got a smoothie from Oakberry. Then I decided to
walk around a little more to find coffee.

That turned into an hour-long walk to Muy Río, where I learned they had run out
of filter coffee 45 minutes before closing. I gave up on coffee, but the walk
ended up taking me right past Casa de Pilatos. I did not go inside because I
was still on a coffee mission that had not officially failed yet.

Afterward, I crossed the Puente de Triana and walked around Triana looking at
ceramics. Centro Cerámica Triana was closing soon, so I did not waste my money
trying to rush through it. I hoped I would have time to come back.

I ate dinner at the hotel that night, and it was one of the top five meals of
my life. I still need to get the names of the dishes. Somehow I found enough
energy to go back out one more time for ice cream at Sibaris.

![Two dishes from dinner at Only YOU Hotel Sevilla](/images/2026/europe/hotel-dinner.jpg)

### Transit cards and cake

I stayed up way too late again, slept until noon, and missed the hotel
breakfast buffet.

My first task for the meetup was to get transit cards for the team. I rode the
bus to Prado de San Sebastián and went to PAC Prado. After working through a
little communication barrier, we did it. I left with the cards and decided to
walk back to the hotel.

Later, I went to Cafetería Tartería Chanticream for queso con fresa and Muerte
por Chocolate cakes with an espresso, then walked to the Setas de Sevilla. I
got there around sunset and stayed long enough to watch the whole structure
change as its lights came on.

![The Setas de Sevilla against a clear evening sky](/images/2026/europe/setas-de-sevilla-arrival.jpg)

![Sunset beneath the Setas de Sevilla](/images/2026/europe/setas-de-sevilla-sunset.jpg)

![Blue lights illuminating the Setas de Sevilla after sunset](/images/2026/europe/setas-de-sevilla-lights.jpg)

On the walk home I took a picture of a random European street at night. That
was apparently the theme of my photography for the day: giant wooden landmark,
giant wooden landmark, giant wooden landmark, street.

![A Seville street under the moon on the walk home from the Setas](/images/2026/europe/seville-night-walk.jpg)

### Back to Triana

The next day I took the tram instead of walking or taking the bus. I finally
got a decent filter coffee at Jester Coffee, then went back to Triana. This time
I made it to Centro Cerámica Triana with enough time to visit the museum. I
also bought a couple of tiles, because carrying fragile ceramics around Europe
for another two weeks seemed like a good idea.

A lot of what caught my attention inside was ceramic being used as graphic
design: advertisements, lettering, and public signs made out of tile.

![A General tire advertisement made from ceramic tiles at Centro Cerámica Triana](/images/2026/europe/ceramica-general-tire-ad.jpg)

![A yellow and green Pirelli tire advertisement made from ceramic tiles](/images/2026/europe/ceramica-pirelli-tire-ad.jpg)

![A wall of historic ceramic advertisements and public signs](/images/2026/europe/ceramica-signs.jpg)

### Plaza de España

The next day I walked from the hotel to NYC Icy and got lotus and chocolate
ice cream, then kept going to Plaza de España. It is a pretty sweet building
and plaza. The whole place is full of ceramic and stone details that are easy
to miss while looking at the scale of it.

![A large ceramic castle medallion at Plaza de España](/images/2026/europe/plaza-espana-castle-medallion.jpg)

![A small castle tile set into the floor at Plaza de España](/images/2026/europe/plaza-espana-castle-tile.jpg)

The stonework was just as good. Someone cut a bow-tie-shaped joint directly
into this railing, and I probably spent longer looking at it than any normal
person would.

![A bow-tie-shaped joint cut into the stone railing at Plaza de España](/images/2026/europe/plaza-espana-stone-joint.jpg)

The downside was that all the carriage rides made the plaza smell like horse
shit, especially with the temperature in the high 30s C.

That makes one week away from home. I have made it to Seville, figured out the
transit, found the coffee, and bought breakable souvenirs far too early in the
trip. The meetup is next.

Gracias por leer.
